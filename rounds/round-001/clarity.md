# Clarity & Exposition — Round 1

**Recommendation:** needs revision
The WHAT→WHY→HOW→WHAT-ELSE arc is sound and the terminology is disciplined, but the flagship section re-derives a third of its own opening from section 1, its central hands-on claim is asserted rather than shown, and one citation is misattached to the term it sits next to.

## Strengths
- The document commits to and delivers its own roadmap: `01-context.prose.md` § What this tutorial covers previews Motivation → Content → Conclusion by name, and each section actually lands where it said it would — a reader is never surprised by what comes next.
- Terms are introduced once and reused without drift: "DFT," "ASE," "foundation model," and "committee" are each expanded or defined at first use and never quietly redefined afterward.
- `02-motivation.prose.md` § When to reach for it earns its keep by bounding the claim honestly (band gaps, exotic bonding, "a handful of DFT calculations would simply be cheaper") rather than only selling MACE — this is the strongest single passage in the document for a skeptical reader.
- The fine-tuning → committee → active learning → agentic-loop chain in `03-content.prose.md` builds cleanly, each `##` unit using only terms the previous one just established, and the closing line of § MACE as a tool in an agentic discovery loop ("This is the loop that was out of reach when the only fast option was inaccurate and the only accurate option was slow") pays off § What goes wrong without it from Motivation almost verbatim — a genuine through-line, not a coincidence.

## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Atoms, energy, and forces and § From a quantum calculation to a machine-learned shortcut re-derive, almost point for point, what `sections/01-context.prose.md` § What this is and § Where it came from already said. Compare: context says "Molecular dynamics simulates how atoms move by repeatedly asking two questions: how much potential energy... and what force..."; content opens "Every arrangement of atoms has a potential energy... The force on any one atom is how steeply that energy would drop..." Same for DFT: context's "DFT... accurate enough to trust but far too slow to repeat at every timestep. Machine-learned interatomic potentials close that gap" versus content's "DFT is accurate, but it solves a genuinely hard problem from scratch every time it is asked... A machine-learned interatomic potential is a shortcut around that cost." Neither passage signals it is a recap, so a reader who just read § Where it came from experiences déjà vu rather than reinforcement, and two of `03-content`'s seven `##` units — the section topic.md explicitly says should carry the weight — are spent restating section 1.
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` § Where it came from cites `[@behler2007generalized]` immediately after "density functional theory (DFT)" — but Behler & Parrinello 2007 is the founding Behler–Parrinello neural-network *potential* paper, not a DFT reference. The citation is attached to the wrong noun phrase; a reader who follows it to learn about DFT lands on a machine-learned-potential paper instead. Compounding this, `01-context.concepts.md` lists Behler–Parrinello by name as part of the stated lineage ("classical force fields → DFT → machine-learned potentials (Behler–Parrinello neural network potentials, GAP, the Atomic Cluster Expansion)"), but the name "Behler–Parrinello" never actually appears in the shipped prose — only this misplaced citation key stands in for it.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Using it: running your first MACE molecular dynamics simulation describes the procedure entirely in prose ("loading a MACE model as a calculator inside ASE... handing it to one of ASE's existing molecular dynamics integrators... a few dozen lines of Python") but shows no code. `topic.md`'s "what the reader will be able to do" states the reader can "Load a pretrained MACE foundation model into ASE and run a molecular dynamics simulation with it" — a procedural claim — and `03-content.concepts.md`'s own open question ("How much ASE/Python code to show verbatim vs. describe") was never closed with a stated decision. As written, the section teaches *that* this is a few dozen lines, not *how*.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` carries seven `##` units — nearly double the four the template ships with — but has no internal signpost the way the whole document does in `01-context`. A reader arriving at "Atoms, energy, and forces" has no sense that four more units (two bridges, mental model, two worked examples, agentic tie-in, pitfalls) are coming before Conclusion.

## Actionable
1. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Atoms, energy, and forces / § From a quantum calculation to a machine-learned shortcut — compress both into a one- or two-sentence callback to `01-context` (e.g., "Recall: MD needs energy and forces recomputed at every step, and DFT is too slow to supply them directly") and move straight to what's new — the shortcut model itself. (*why:* recovers real estate in the section meant to carry the tutorial's weight, and removes the déjà-vu of reading the same claim twice with no acknowledgment.)
2. `sections/01-context.prose.md` § Where it came from — move `[@behler2007generalized]` off "density functional theory (DFT)" and onto the machine-learned-potentials sentence where Behler–Parrinello actually belongs, and name it in text ("...neural-network potentials [@behler2007generalized], early versions [@bartok2010gap]...") so the lineage the spine promises is the lineage the prose delivers. (*why:* a citation attached to the wrong term actively misleads a reader who follows it, and currently the one name topic.md's lineage claim rests on never appears.)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Using it: running your first MACE molecular dynamics simulation — add a short, real code excerpt (loading the MACE-MP-0 calculator, attaching it to the water molecule, running one MD integrator) even if only 10–15 lines, or explicitly reframe the section's claim to "describes the shape of the workflow" rather than implying the reader can now do it. (*why:* topic.md and the audience standard both promise the reader can run a simulation after this tutorial — a description of code is not the same capability as having seen the code.)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` — add one sentence at the top of the section naming its own shape (two bridges, the mental model, two worked cases, the agentic tie-in, then pitfalls), mirroring what `01-context` already does for the whole document. (*why:* the section is now the size of the rest of the document combined; a reader benefits from the same roadmap courtesy extended to it.)
